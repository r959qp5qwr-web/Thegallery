Researched 2026-11-09 (read-only). No repository was edited.

## 1. Art and craft discovery and marketplace apps (mobile)

### Artsy (iOS/Android)
- App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/artsy-buy-sell-fine-art/id703796080 [SEEN]
- Mobbin, purchasing flow: https://mobbin.com/explore/flows/052046c8-aa9d-47e8-a365-650a4dd90023 [SEEN-URL]
- Mobbin, feature info screen: https://mobbin.com/explore/screens/048fe9a2-3792-4762-acec-a16cea8ef19f [SEEN-URL]
- Mobbin, AR "Artwork Placement" (view in room): https://mobbin.com/explore/screens/f963651f-11e6-4d44-92d1-4e5aac59236b [SEEN-URL]
- Patterns:
  - The artwork screen opens with the image at full width on a white ground. Below it sit the artist name (bold), the title and year (italic), and then medium, size and price in small grey type. A sticky full-width black primary button sits at the bottom ("Purchase" or "Contact gallery").
  - Bottom tab bar with 5 icons (Home, Search, Inbox, Sell, Profile). A heart and a share icon sit in the top-right of the artwork screen.
  - Home is a vertical stack of horizontal carousel "rails". Each rail has a one-line title and a "See all" link. The cards are image-first, with 2 to 3 caption lines under each.
  - An artwork screen has roughly 40 to 70 words above the fold (estimate).

### Are.na (iOS/Android/web)
- App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/are-na/id1299153149 [SEEN]. Play: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=na.are.aredotna&hl=en_US [SEEN]. Web: https://www.are.na/ [SEEN]
- Design critique (Pratt IXD): https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/02/design-critique-are-na-ios-app/ [SEEN]
- Patterns:
  - A square block grid in which every item is the same size: images, text and links alike. Channel titles are set in a single serif or grotesk, with very little colour.
  - The calm, ad-free tone is a positioning statement in its own right. The interface stays out of the way.
  - Useful as a counter-reference: precisely because it is text-tolerant, it reads more "tool" than "gallery". Borrow its grid discipline, not its density.

### Pinterest (iOS/Android)
- Mobbin, pins grid: https://mobbin.com/explore/screens/181bb372-a7b0-45ef-8f17-521399c3eff8 [SEEN-URL]

## 2. Museums and galleries with a strong typographic identity

### David Zwirner (web; the strongest direct reference for the work screen)
- Home: https://www.davidzwirner.com/ [SEEN, scraped]
- Artwork page: https://www.davidzwirner.com/artworks/katherine-bradford-divers-8dab2 [SEEN, scraped at mobile]
- Patterns, observed in the scrape:
  - The artwork page reads, in order: the image, then "Artist  Title, Year" as one heading, then 6 short metadata lines (medium, size, edition, printer, publisher, signature), then a second image view, then "View All by artist", then one action, "Inquire".
  - About 45 to 55 words in total on the artwork page (observed, excluding the cookie and newsletter chrome).
  - The home page is mostly a grid of images with almost no captions.
- Takeaway: "Inquire" maps directly onto "Message on WhatsApp". One action, no cart.

### Gagosian (web)
- https://gagosian.com/ [SEEN, scraped at mobile]
- Patterns, observed:
  - The hero is a slider of full-bleed exhibition images. Each slide overlays a 4-line text block: "Now on View / Artist / Title / Location", then "Learn More".
  - "On View" cards are a square thumbnail, the artist in bold, the title, the dates and the city.
  - Lower down, the home page becomes long-form editorial ("Quarterly", news). The top screen is image-first, and the page as a whole has more than 1,500 words.
- Counter-lesson: The Gallery's home should be like Gagosian's first screen only. Do not continue into editorial below it.

### Bloomberg Connects (the arts and culture app used by MoMA, the Met, Serpentine and others)
- Site: https://www.bloombergconnects.org/ [SEEN]. App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/connects-arts-culture/id1476456847 [SEEN]. Play: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.bloomberg.connects.docent&hl=en_US [SEEN]
- Guide list (includes MoMA, the Met, Whitney, Serpentine and the Courtauld): https://www.bloombergconnects.org/guides/ [SEEN]
- Patterns:
  - Each institution gets its own "guide" with its own cover image and identity inside a shared app shell. This is the closest structural analogue to "each maker gets their own gallery inside The Gallery".
  - Guide screens show a cover image, then tiles for Highlights, Exhibitions and Lookup number. Object screens show the image, the title, then audio or text.
  - App Store reviews cite state loss when switching apps. The lesson for a PWA: preserve scroll position and state when the visitor returns from WhatsApp. [SEEN in the review excerpt]

### Rijksmuseum app (Rijksstudio on the web)
- App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rijksmuseum/id621307961 [SEEN]. Play: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=nl.rijksmuseum.mmt&hl=en_US [SEEN]. App page: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/whats-on/app [SEEN]
- Patterns:
  - Masterpiece imagery at full bleed. Deep zoom on artworks (pinch to the brushstroke). Number lookup for objects in the galleries.
  - Users on a travel forum describe it as "one of the best museum apps out there". [SEEN in the search excerpt]
  - Zoom-to-detail is worth borrowing for craft: glaze, weave and grain are the point of the work.

### Smartify (museum app)
- https://smartify.org/ [SEEN]. App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/smartify-arts-and-culture/id1102736524 [SEEN]

## Shared conventions that make something read as an app on a phone
1. **Persistent bottom tab bar** with 3–5 icons, labels optional (Artsy, Pinterest, Instagram, SSENSE, Cosmos's floating capsule).
2. **Image-first feeds** in a 2-column masonry or 3-column grid, with captions absent or one line long (Pinterest, Cosmos, Instagram).
3. **Full-bleed media** on detail screens, with swipe paging and dots or pinch-to-zoom (Etsy, Rijksmuseum, Pinterest).
4. **Sticky primary action** anchored to the bottom, full width or as a pill (Artsy "Purchase/Contact", Pinterest "Save", Zwirner "Inquire").
5. **Bottom sheets** for secondary detail and actions instead of new pages (Mobbin's bottom-sheet index; Shop's product detail).
6. **Accordions or "more" sections** that hide the long tail of detail (Shop product detail).
7. **Cards with consistent radius and spacing**; rails of horizontal carousels on home (Artsy, Lake).
8. **Icon buttons in place of text links**: heart, share, back chevron, overflow menu (Pinterest, Artsy).
9. **Large tap targets** (44pt or more) and chips or segmented controls for filters (Etsy listing, Cosmos chips).
10. **One task per screen in setup**, with progress and Skip (Shopify, Instagram profile setup, Linktree).
11. **State that survives**: returning from another app lands you where you left off (the Bloomberg Connects reviews show the cost of not doing this).
12. **Branded splash or intro made only of imagery** (Cosmos intro).

## (a) The 10 strongest references for The Gallery, ranked
1. **Instagram profile grid**: the maker gallery page. Avatar, name, short bio, one Message button, and a dense 3-column grid of works. This is the familiar mental model for visitors arriving from WhatsApp.
2. **David Zwirner artwork page**: the work screen. Image, then "Artist, Title, Year", then 5–6 metadata lines, then one "Inquire" action, in about 50 words (observed).
3. **Cosmos**: the visitor's browsing feel. Captionless masonry, a dark canvas, an image-only intro and a floating icon bar. This is where "cutting edge" lives.
4. **Artsy app**: the structure of an art marketplace on a phone. Rails on home, a sticky bottom CTA, save and share icons, and a 5-tab bar.
5. **Shopify onboarding**: maker setup. One question per screen, and the user sees their own store before the account is finished.
6. **Bloomberg Connects**: the platform-of-many-galleries shell. Every institution or maker keeps its own identity inside one app.
7. **Pinterest**: save-first interaction. A Save pill on the detail screen, "more like this" continuing below, and a tab bar without labels.
8. **Linktree onboarding**: claiming the shareable link is the first act. The analogue is atthegallery.in/<maker>.
9. **Rijksmuseum app**: deep zoom on artwork detail, which matters for craft surfaces (glaze, weave, grain).
10. **SSENSE**: typographic restraint. Monochrome, small caps labels and a caption of 2 lines at most.
(Honourable mentions: Etsy for the image carousel and "Message seller"; Gagosian's first screen for full-bleed hero cards with overlaid text; The Registry of Sarees for the local cultural tone.)

## (b) Design principles derived from the references
1. **The work is the interface.** Feeds show images with no captions, or with one line at most (Cosmos, Pinterest).
2. **A maker's page is a profile grid, not an article.** Avatar, name, a bio of about 150 characters at most, one action, and a 3-column grid (Instagram).
3. **One work screen, one action.** Image, then maker and title, then 3–5 facts, then a sticky "Message on WhatsApp" (Zwirner "Inquire", Artsy sticky CTA).
4. **Primary actions are anchored at the bottom, within thumb reach.** Sticky full-width or pill buttons (Artsy, Pinterest Save).
5. **Navigation is a bottom tab bar of 3–4 icons.** No hamburger menu for primary destinations (Artsy, Pinterest, Instagram).
6. **Media goes full-bleed on detail screens, with swipe and pinch.** Photos run edge to edge, with paging dots and zoom (Etsy, Rijksmuseum).
7. **Secondary detail lives in sheets and accordions.** Care notes, dimensions and process collapse by default (Shop product detail, Mobbin bottom-sheet patterns).
8. **Saving is a one-tap icon, and saves get their own tab.** A heart on the image, plus a "Saved" destination (Pinterest, Smartify, Artsy).
9. **Typography is a small, strict system.** One sans for the interface with small caps labels, and an optional display face only for maker names. No body paragraphs on primary screens (SSENSE, Zwirner, KNMA's large type).
10. **Onboarding asks one question per screen, with a visible payoff.** Show the maker their gallery with their first photo before asking for more (Shopify, Squarespace's template-first flow).
11. **Choose by tapping pictures, not by typing.** The craft category is chosen from image tiles (Spotify artist picker, Cosmos chips, Linktree categories).
12. **Claim the link early.** The shareable URL is the reward and the hook (Linktree).
13. **Each maker keeps their identity inside a shared shell.** The platform chrome stays quiet so each gallery's cover and works lead (Bloomberg Connects).
14. **State survives app switches.** Returning from WhatsApp restores scroll position and the open work (the Bloomberg Connects review shows the failure mode).
15. **The brand voice is terse.** Two-word buttons, titles of 3–6 words and no explanatory paragraphs (Cargo home at about 8 words, Cosmos "Skip intro").

## (c) Typical word counts on screen
| Screen | Best-reference range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Home or discovery (first viewport) | **0–30 words** (Cosmos and Pinterest feeds about 0–10; Artsy home rails about 20–40, rail titles plus captions; Gagosian hero slide about 10–15 per slide; Cargo home about 8) | Gagosian and Cargo observed. Cosmos, Pinterest and Artsy estimated. |
| Product or work screen (above the fold) | **15–55 words** (Zwirner about 45–55 in total, observed; Artsy about 40–70 including price and metadata, estimate; Pinterest pin about 5–20, estimate) | Mixed |
| Onboarding screen | **5–20 words** (title of 3–6 words, at most 1 helper line of about 8–12 words, button label of 1–2 words; Shop runs 19 screens in about 73 seconds, roughly 4 seconds each) | Shop timings observed. Linktree, Shopify and Instagram counts estimated. |

Practical targets for The Gallery: home at 30 words or fewer above the fold, a work screen at 40 words or fewer above the fold, onboarding steps at 15 words or fewer, and button labels of 1–3 words.
